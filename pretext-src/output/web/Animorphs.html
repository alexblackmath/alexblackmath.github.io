    Subsection 1.1 Isomorphism

    This is an important fact that we have used implicitly in class that I want you to work through explicitly as an exercise:
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.1.

    Let \(S \subseteq V\) be a linearly independent subset of a vector space. Then \(S\) is a basis for \(\text{span}(S)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.2.

    Let \(T: U \to V\) be a linear transformation. Let \(B\) be a basis for \(U\text{.}\) Suppose that \(\ker(T) = \{\vec{0}\}\text{.}\) Show that \(T(B) = \{T(\vec{v}): \vec{v} \in B\}\) is a basis for \(\text{Im}(T)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    🔗
    🔗
    We call a linear transformation an isomorphism if it is also a bijection. That is, if it is both one-to-one and onto.
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.3.

    Let \(U\) and \(V\) be finite dimensional vector spaces of the same dimension. Then let \(T: U \to V\text{.}\) Show that if \(\text{rank}(T) = \text{dim}(U)\text{,}\) then \(T\) is a linear isomorphism.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.

    Checkpoint 1.5 Expected profit from repeated investor pitches.

    An entrepreneur repeatedly pitches a business idea to potential investors. Each pitch is successful with probability \(1/5\) independently of all previous pitches. The entrepreneur stops pitching as soon as the first successful pitch occurs. Each pitch costs $200 to prepare and deliver. If a pitch is successful, the entrepreneur receives $8,000 in funding. Let \(N\) be the random variable denoting the total number of pitches made, including the successful pitch.
    🔗
    (a)
    Find \(\mathbb{E}[N]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Let \(Y\) be the random variable denoting the entrepreneur’s net profit after the first successful pitch. Express \(Y\) in terms of \(N\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Compute \(\mathbb{E}[Y]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Suppose instead that each pitch costs $1,000. Compute the entrepreneur’s expected net profit.
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    More generally, suppose each pitch costs $\(c\text{.}\) Find the value of \(c\) for which the entrepreneur’s expected net profit is exactly zero.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.6.

    Subdivide a circle of radius \(R\) centered at \((0,0)\) into cells. Suppose that the expected length of the intersection of any line through \((0,0)\) with any given cell is at least \(L\text{.}\) Show that the expected total number of cells intersected by a ray emanating from \((0,0)\) is at most \(2R/L\text{.}\)
