About the geometry and the source

Figure 4, animated

Based on Any Proof of Polynomial Hirsch Must Be Completely Incoherent, Alexander E. Black and Lei Xue, Figure 4 on page 17. The opening follows the three triangular cells in the figure; the extension to three nested copies follows the construction in Lemmas 3.10–3.13 and Theorem 3.14.

The paper’s figure is schematic. The coordinates and the particular triangulation here are chosen for this animation; this is not a numerical reconstruction of the high-dimensional example. Every drawing is a horizontal slice H = {z = 1} of a normal-fan picture, and the marked point represents e₃.

A narrow 42° cone is mapped to each of three 120° sectors. The union of the three cones covers the plane; no single proper cone is transformed into all of space. Each deformation uses continuous rotation and positive anisotropic scaling, so its matrix is invertible throughout. The entire fan and its rays are transformed together.

The copies are nested by a strict homothety of approximately 0.068666 at each level. The blue connections are computed from a compatible regular triangulation (a lower convex hull of explicitly lifted vertices), not drawn arbitrarily. It has 15 vertices, 21 original red edges, 16 new blue edges, and 23 triangles. The construction preserves every original edge, introduces no new vertices, has no crossings, and fills the outer support exactly. Its regular lifting and geometric checks are included in the source package.

The animation is calculated continuously by JavaScript and drawn entirely as SVG paths, circles, gradients and text. It contains no generated images or baked frame sequence. The camera’s closing zoom is continuous in logarithmic scale. The final two magnified views show the smaller copies without altering their relative sizes in the main construction.
