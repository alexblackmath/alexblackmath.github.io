    Subsection 1.1 Playing the Game

    Checkpoint 1.1.

    For each of the following pairs of points, find a \(2 \times 2\) matrix taking the first point to the second.
    🔗
    (a)
    \((1,2)\) to \((2,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \((0,0)\) to \((1,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \((1,0)\) to \((0,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \((-1,-1)\) to \((1,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Recall from Math 1800 the geometry of vector addition. Namely, given \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\text{,}\) the points \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((a,b)\text{,}\) \((a+c,b+d)\text{,}\) and \((c,d)\) are the vertices of a (possibly degenerate) parallelogram. For example, if \((a,b)=(1,0)\) and \((c,d)=(0,1)\text{,}\) the parallelogram has vertices \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((1,0)\text{,}\) \((1,1)\text{,}\) and \((0,1)\text{,}\) and is therefore a square.
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.2.

    What property of a linear transformation guarantees that, given the vertices \(\vec{0}\text{,}\) \(\vec{u}\text{,}\) \(\vec{v}\text{,}\) and \(\vec{u}+\vec{v}\) of a parallelogram and a \(2 \times 2\) matrix \(A\text{,}\) the points \(\vec{0}\text{,}\) \(A\vec{u}\text{,}\) \(A\vec{v}\text{,}\) and \(A(\vec{u}+\vec{v})\) are also the vertices of a parallelogram?

    Subsection 1.2 Matrices of Linear Transformations

    Checkpoint 1.3.

    Suppose I have a linear transformation that scales all coordinates by a factor of \(2\text{:}\) \(T(\vec{x})=2\vec{x}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (a)
    If \(T:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\text{,}\) what is the matrix of \(T\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    If \(T:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\text{,}\) what is the matrix of \(T\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Describe in words what you think such a matrix would look like in general.

    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.4.

    Consider the linear transformation
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} T\left(\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}-y\\x\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    (a)
    Compute
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} T\left(\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\right). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    (b)
    Find the matrix of the linear transformation \(T\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Experiment with applying the linear transformation to examples using the following website. Describe in your own words what this linear transformation does geometrically.
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Based on your description in part (c), what do you expect \(T^{2}\) to do?
