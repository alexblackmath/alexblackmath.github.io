    Subsection 1.1 Vectors, Independence, and Span

    Checkpoint 1.1.

    Consider the following vectors
    \begin{equation*} \vec{a} = (x+y, x+z, y+z) \end{equation*}
    and
    \begin{equation*} \vec{b} = (x, 1+x+y, x+y+z)\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Do the following:
    🔗
    (a)
    Rewrite \(\vec{a}\) in the form \(x\vec{v}_{1} + y \vec{v}_{2} + z \vec{v}_{3} + \vec{v}_{4}\text{,}\) where \(\vec{v}_{1}, \vec{v}_{2}, \vec{v}_{3},\) and \(\vec{v}_{4}\) all have entries in \(\mathbb{R}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Rewrite \(\vec{b}\) in the form \(x\vec{v}_{1} + y \vec{v}_{2} + z \vec{v}_{3} + \vec{v}_{4}\text{,}\) where \(\vec{v}_{1}, \vec{v}_{2}, \vec{v}_{3},\) and \(\vec{v}_{4}\) all have entries in \(\mathbb{R}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Rewrite \(\vec{a} + \vec{b}\) in the form \(x\vec{v}_{1} + y \vec{v}_{2} + z \vec{v}_{3} + \vec{v}_{4}\text{,}\) where \(\vec{v}_{1}, \vec{v}_{2}, \vec{v}_{3},\) and \(\vec{v}_{4}\) all have entries in \(\mathbb{R}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    We say vectors are linearly independent if there is no linear combination of them with nonzero coefficients that equals \(\vec{0}\text{.}\) For example, \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) are linearly independent, since if \(x(1,0) + y(0,1) = (0,0)\text{,}\) then \(x = y = 0\text{.}\) However, \((1,0) \text{,}\) \((0,1)\text{,}\) and \((2,2)\) are not linear independent, because \(2(1,0)+ 2(0,1) - (2,2) = (0,0)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.2.

    Consider the vector equation:
    \begin{equation*} x_{1} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{pmatrix} + x_{2} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1\\ 1\end{pmatrix} + x_{3} \begin{pmatrix}1 \\ 1\\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    (a)
    Rewrite this is a system of linear equations.
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Solve the linear system.
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Based on your solution to part (b), are \((1,0,1)\text{,}\) \((0,1,1)\text{,}\) and \((1,1,0)\) linearly independent?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.3.

    Is \((1,0,1,0)\) in the span of \((1,1,0,0)\text{,}\) \((1,0,0,1)\text{,}\) and \((0,1,1,0)\text{?}\) Explain why or why not.
    🔗

    Subsection 1.2 Matrix Form

    Checkpoint 1.4.

    Consider the matrix
    \begin{equation*} A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 3 &3& 4 & 4\end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Consider the vectors
    \begin{equation*} \vec{v}_{1} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{pmatrix}, \vec{v}_{2} =\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \\ 1 \\ -3\end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    (a)
    Compute \(A \vec{v}_{1}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Compute \(\vec{v}_{1} + \vec{v}_{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Compute \(2 \vec{v}_{1}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Compute \(2A \vec{v}_{1}\) and \(A (2 \vec{v}_{1})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    Compute \(A \vec{v}_{1} +A\vec{v}_{2}\) and \(A(\vec{v}_{1} + \vec{v}_{2})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.5.

    Consider the matrix equation:
    \begin{equation*} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y\\ z \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    (a)
    Rewrite this as a system of linear equations.
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Solve the system of linear equations.
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Use your solution to describe the plane spanned by \((1,1,0)\) and \((1,0,1)\) by a linear equation.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.6.

    Consider the \(2\)-plane in \(\mathbb{R}^{4}\) spanned by \((1,0,1,0)\) and \((0,1,0,1)\text{.}\) Write this plane as a solution set to a system of linear equations.
