The idea behind the game

A shorter distance is not the objective. Count tile interiors, starting with the tile at the origin.

—
—

Why the two escape problems are different The mathematics

One score, carefully defined.

The starting tile counts as one. A ray scores the number of distinct tiles whose interiors it crosses for positive length. A free route scores tile visits; each neighbor move adds one, including revisits. The final step outside adds no tile.

Turning can only help.

Rays through tile vertices, including where edges meet the rim, are forbidden. Following a tile edge is forbidden too. Every legal ray traces shared-edge neighbors. Every cell is convex, so a ray cannot leave a tile and re-enter it. Its ordered tile sequence is itself a legal free route with the same score. So the shortest free route is always at most the score of any legal ray.

How “best possible” is found.

Free routes use breadth-first search on the tile-adjacency graph. Ray counts are checked once in each open angular interval between consecutive vertex directions, including circle-edge intersections. Vertex directions themselves are excluded. A tiny numerical guard around them prevents floating-point corner shortcuts.

The rules

Two ways out. One origin.

Every level gives you the same convex-cell subdivision for two challenges. You always begin at the white origin, strictly inside a tile. Your score is measured in tiles, not physical distance.

A · Ray: choose a direction

Move your pointer, tap the circle, or use the angle slider. The lit tiles are your preview. Press Launch ray to submit. A tile counts only when the ray crosses its interior for positive length; merely touching it does not count. Every tile is convex, so a ray cannot leave a tile and re-enter it. Rays through any tile vertex, including a vertex on the rim, or along a tile edge are forbidden. “Avoid vertices” nudges a forbidden aim to a nearby legal direction; turning that assistance off never makes vertex rays legal.

B · Free: choose your neighbors

Click a highlighted neighboring tile, or use the labeled buttons. A move must cross a shared edge, not just a corner. When your tile reaches a nonzero arc of the rim, press Escape the circle. Each tile visit costs one; moving inside a tile is free. Undo lets you revise your route.

The starting tile counts as 1. Your last step into the exterior adds 0. “Best possible” is revealed after your first completed escape in each part. Match it to earn a mastery star. Before your first guess, hints offer strategy without revealing a solution. Hints are marked on saved results. You can replay freely, switch parts, and jump to any level.

Keyboard (with the board focused): In Ray mode, ← / → turn by 1°; hold Shift for 0.1°. ↑ / ↓ select the next / previous safe direction between vertices. Enter launches. In Free mode, ← / → cycle neighboring tiles, and Enter moves or escapes. U undoes; R restarts the current part; H gives a hint. Native inputs keep their normal keyboard behavior.
