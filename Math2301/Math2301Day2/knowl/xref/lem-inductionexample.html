Lemma 2.3.

Let \(V\) be a vector space. Then any finite linear combination of elements of \(V\) is in \(V\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
To prove this, we proceed by induction. Let \(\vec{u} \in V\text{,}\) and suppose that \(\vec{u}\) is a linear combination of \(1\) element of \(V\text{.}\) Then \(\vec{u} = \lambda \vec{v}\) for some \(\vec{v} \in V\) and \(\lambda \in \mathbb{R}\text{.}\) Since \(V\) is closed under scaling and \(\vec{v} \in V\) \(\lambda \vec{v} \in V\text{.}\)
🔗
Suppose for the sake of induction that any linear combination of \(m\) elements of \(V\) is in \(V\text{.}\) We want to show that any linear combination of \(m+1\) elements of \(V\) must be in \(V\text{.}\) Let
\begin{equation*} \vec{u} = \sum_{i=1}^{m+1} a_{i} \vec{v}_{i} \end{equation*}
be such a linear combination so that \(a_{1}, \dots, a_{m+1} \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(\vec{v}_{1}, \dots, \vec{v}_{m+1} \in V\text{.}\) Let
\begin{equation*} \vec{w} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{i} \vec{v}_{i}. \end{equation*}
Then \(\vec{w}\) is a linear combination of \(m\) elements of \(V\text{,}\) so by our inductive hypothesis, \(\vec{w} \in V\text{.}\) Furthermore, since \(V\) is closed under scaling \(a_{m+1}\vec{v}_{m+1} \in V\text{.}\) Then, since \(V\) is closed under addition
\begin{equation*} \vec{u} = \sum_{i=1}^{m+1} a_{i} \vec{v}_{i} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{i} \vec{v}_{i} + a_{m+1} \vec{v}_{m+1} = \vec{u} + a_{m+1} \vec{v}_{m+1} \in V. \end{equation*}
Therefore, any finite linear combination of elements of \(V\) is in \(V\text{.}\)
