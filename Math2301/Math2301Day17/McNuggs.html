    Subsection 1.5 Subset-Sum

    The chicken nugget problem seems silly, but similar questions appear in computer science as fundamental subroutines in more complicated computational problems. For example, they have a limited stock of nuggets and they may already be prepackaged.
    🔗

    Checkpoint 1.9.

    Say you’re at Chick-fil-A and they have
    • 6 packs of 5 nuggs left
      🔗
    • 1 pack of 8 nuggs left
      🔗
    • 2 packs of 12 nuggs left
      🔗
    • 1 pack of 30 nuggs left
      🔗
    🔗
    (a)
    Can I get 42 nuggs?
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Can I get 50 nuggs?
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Can I get \(100\) nuggs?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    These are examples of the subset sum problem in theoretical computer science. Namely, you want an algorithm to solve the following problem: Given a list of numbers \(x_{1}, x_{2}, \dots, x_{n}\) and a number \(C\text{,}\) can I find a subset that sums to exactly \(C\text{?}\)
    🔗
    This is really a linear algebra question. Equivalently, can you find \(y_{1}, y_{2}, \dots, y_{n}\) all either \(0\) or \(1\) such that
    \begin{equation*} \vec{y} \cdot \vec{x} = C? \end{equation*}
    This is a single linear equation! If \(\vec{y}\) could have entries given by any real number, we know how to solve this. In general, this problem is actually provably hard. If you could prove you can solve it efficiently, you would literally get a million dollars. That’s how much of a difference restricting coefficients makes. Amazingly though you can use linear algebra as a tool to help solve this in practice.
    🔗

    Subsection 1.6 Independence

    What you can observe is that sometimes some of the options are redundant:

    Checkpoint 1.10.

    Which sizes of mcnuggs are not needed to be able to generate new possibilities? What about for Chick-fil-A?

    You can ask the same question for vectors in any vector space. Given a set of vectors, \(\vec{v}_{1}, \dots, \vec{v}_{n}\text{,}\) are there any that we can throw out without changing the span? If there are none, we call the vectors linearly independent. This happens precisely when I can’t get any of the vectors of the list by combining other vectors.
